## Structure (home: `references/structure.md`)

- **Main clause carries the main news** — sentence-initial subordinator + vivid action, then a weak/stative main clause.
- **Main-clause X-ray and rebuild** — subject+verb strips to "X is Y" tautology; abstract subject + of-phrase; emerges as/serves as/stands as.
- **Unbury nominalizations** — carrier verb (have/make/show/achieve) + -tion/-ment/-ness/-ity noun; nominalized subject + empty verb (took place, was evident).
- **Kill "the + gerund + of"** — literal "the [verb]-ing of"; doer-less gerund subject + vague predicate.
- **Un-stuff "has + noun + participle"** — have/has/had + noun + -ing verb carrying the real news.
- **Unstick clause-as-noun subjects** — "That.../The fact that..." + "is the reason that / is why / is what."
- **Collapse empty existentials and clefts** — "There is/are X who/that..." or purposeless "It was X that..." → promote the real subject and verb.
- **True-passive test** — be-form + next word: -ing or adjective = active; transitive past participle + "by [someone]" fits = passive.
- **Activate action-squelching passives** — be + vivid transitive participle, actor in by-phrase or missing, in narrative prose.
- **Keep the passive to downplay the doer** — receiver is the topic; active subject would be a vague crowd; is considered / is regarded as.
- **Restructure passive pileups** — 2+ be+participle constructions chained; stacked "had been X-ed" auxiliaries.
- **Coordinate equals, subordinate to encode** — subordinator joining equal-value clauses; "and" hiding a causal/temporal link.
- **"As" means simultaneous** — "as" chaining sequential events; reaction-shot main clause with the real event in the as-clause.
- **Definition traps: while/since/if/than** — concessive "while," causal "since" after a completed event, "if" before a standing fact, comparative "than" + bare noun.
- **Parse units before moving them** — fragments; stranded adverbs/adjectives; split preposition-object teams; over-corrected imperatives and infinitives.

## Reader-first (home: `references/reader-first.md`)

- **Serve the Reader:** self-pity, insider refs, jargon, verdict words → answer "really saying?" / "why care?"
- **Lead With Reader Impact:** [institution] + voted/announced/approved with consequence in trailing clause
- **Main Point in Main Clause:** trivial main clause; pivotal action in stacked participial/subordinate phrases
- **Uncram:** 30+ words, 3+ chained clauses, tacked-on ", which...", repeated prepositions, stacked time elements
- **Brevity/Mix:** craft avg >~25 words/sentence; or 4+ same-shape short sentences droning
- **Don't Bury the Verb:** >~8–10 words between subject and main verb
- **No Monster Openers:** first sentence of piece/section >~35 words or convention-defying
- **Let Facts Stand Alone:** emphatic declaratives chained by even though/because; pleading tone
- **No Sleaze Bait:** withheld referents, threat inflation, "details at eleven"
- **Say It Like You'd Speak It:** phrasing that fails the read-aloud test; participial wind-ups
- **Kill Meta-Announcements:** "There were two options." / "It's worth noting that" / restated conclusions
- **Cut Doubled Actions:** "met with X and told him"; verb pairs naming one event
- **Explain Fully or Not at All:** "too different," "various reasons," category-not-fact because-clauses
- **Cut Writer-Serving Modifiers:** show-off words, coy self-censorship, "in my humble opinion" hedges
- **Pare-Down Modifier Test:** literal read of modifier + target; >1 time element per action; repeated prepositions
- **Verify Accuracy and Voice:** run after every rewrite — dropped time markers, flipped sequence, flattened deliberate effects

## Modifiers (home: `references/modifiers.md`)

- **Nearest-Word Attachment** — PP not beside its logical target; a noun intervenes.
- **Un-Dangle Participles** — fronted -ing/-ed phrase + subject that isn't the doer.
- **Possessive Anchor** — fronted modifier + "X's Y" where the modifier describes X.
- **Ambiguous Pronouns** — third-person pronoun with 2+ matching candidate nouns.
- **Vague It/That/Which** — pronoun pointing at a verb, clause, or multi-idea sentence.
- **One List, One Form** — stem + item fails grammar or truth when distributed.
- **Restrictive Commas** — comma presence contradicts the clause-removal test.
- **Relative-Clause Overload** — 2+ that/which/who clauses, or "oh, by the way" backstory.
- **Modifier Pileup** — stacked modifiers where no reordering works; rewrite/split/colon.
- **Trailing-Modifier Scope** — "A, B, and C with X" where both scopes parse.
- **Manner-Adverb Deletion Test** — -ly/degree word on action verb; delete unless it adds meaning.
- **Show, Don't Tell Manner** — vivid verb + intensity adverb; swap for observable result.
- **Gerund/Participle Twins** — -ing + noun with two readings; insert "that" or recast.
- **Suspended Compounds** — bare attributive noun stranded before a shared plural tail.
- **Repeat Function Word** — single "by/to/in" over long or "as well as" items.
- **Ellipsis Check** — "X is, too." where restoration copies a long phrase.
- **Zero Relative** — droppable "that" before its clause's own subject + verb.
- **That/Which Style** — restrictive "which" in AP/Chicago prose.
- **Verify Function** — that/which/who or adverb doing a non-target job; don't misapply rules.

## Concision (home: `references/concision.md`)

- **Specific over opaque:** person / thing(s) / item(s) / structure / place / noise / procedure / get-together / something / some kind of; generic verbs (moved, went, got, implemented)
- **Bold statements:** "one of the more [adj] aspects of X is (the fact) that..."; "has adopted a [noun] that is"; decidedly / arguably
- **He-is-a-man-who:** X + be + man/woman/place/thing/activity + who/that/which
- **Flabby insertions:** in addition to...also; for X's part; in terms of; the question as to whether; due to the fact that; the fact that; in order to; prior to; at this point in time; is able to; it is important to note that
- **Dead-weight adjectives:** renowned / brilliant / awesome before a name; 3+ stacked adjectives
- **Goldbricking adverbs:** very / extremely / totally / currently / already; -ly duplicating the verb (freshly renovated, quickly grabbed); menacingly / cruelly
- **Definition-level redundancy:** modifier entailed by noun (ambient climate); overlap pairs (known to be notoriously, the exact same); circular descriptions
- **Fact-cramming:** parentheticals with numerals/specs or >5 words; 2+ per sentence; "said, suggesting... and adding..."
- **Quoted words are verbatim:** flab inside quotation marks → partial quote, paraphrase, or leave-and-note; never edit in-quote
- **Failed metaphors:** inanimate subjects doing organic actions; stacked figurative verbs; 2+ images on one event; unanswerable literal questions
- **Mushy core:** abstract subject + rare/misused/ambiguous verb (a monotone shirred; drew stains)
- **Literal-meaning audit:** "you needn't X to Y" where X = Y; "the reach/draw/pull of [object-not-possessor]"
- **Invented compounds:** unhyphenated-in-the-wild X-fated / X-doomed / X-bound attributives
- **Part-of-whole:** "the [component] on/of a [whole that includes it]" in comparisons
- **From...to:** legs over a few words; nested from/to; commas or semicolons inside; long intro modifier
- **Attribution:** any quote verb other than said (enthused, extolled); "echoed the sentiment"
- **Semicolons:** joins two independent clauses; "; and"; 3+ per sentence; list items lacking internal commas

## Grammar & mechanics (home: `references/grammar-mechanics.md`)

- **Stacked auxiliaries** — 2+ helpers (had been -ing) with no "another" event → simplest tense.
- **Present verbs on finished events** — completed action, present tense, or unanchored present narration → simple past.
- **Past-perfect chains** — consecutive "had + participle" sentences → anchor once, then simple past.
- **Tense drift** — mixed tenses in one timeframe ("was... who is") → conform.
- **Past-tense timeless facts** — "discovered that X was" for a still-true X → present in the that-clause.
- **Fragment check** — no subject + finite verb between periods → attach or complete (unless deliberate).
- **Subordinator-only sentence** — because/although/when clause standing alone → join to main clause.
- **Run-on / comma splice** — two independent clauses fused or joined by a bare comma → period, comma + conjunction, or subordinate.
- **Copular + -ly** — feel badly / smells terribly → adjective complement.
- **Agreement** — verb number vs. subject head noun; there's + plural; either/or; one-of-the-X-who → §5a routine.
- **Preposition + subject pronoun** — "between you and I" → object form.
- **Demand + conjugated verb / hypothetical "was"** — → subjunctive base form / were.
- **Series comma inconsistency** — both "A, B and C" and "A, B, and C" present → one policy.
- **Stacked-adjective commas** — run and-test + swap-test → add or delete commas.
- **Missing junction comma** — long intro or two joined independent clauses, no comma → insert.
- **Comma pairs** — restrictive with commas / nonrestrictive without / unclosed ", Inc." ", Jr." dates → fix pair.
- **Plural apostrophe** — 's on a word that owns/contracts nothing → delete.
- **Quote-mark order** — period/comma outside quotes, colon/semicolon inside → move; ?/! by logic.
- **Double terminal marks** — "Tex.." or "?"." → keep one.
- **Preposed compound** — ambiguous two-word modifier before noun → hyphenate; never after -ly.
- **Misplaced prepositional phrase** — phrase beside wrong noun → move next to what it modifies.
- **Iconic-error strings** — it's/could of/have went/effect-as-verb/then-than → run the full trigger table.
